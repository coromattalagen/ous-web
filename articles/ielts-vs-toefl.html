IELTS vs TOEFL: Which One Actually Fits You?

OUS Academy Editorial · 10 min read · Updated September 2026

Both IELTS and TOEFL are widely accepted proof of English proficiency. The better choice depends on your destination, your strengths, and the specific requirements of the institutions or organizations you are applying to. This article compares the two tests on the points that matter most for decision-making in 2026.

1. Where the scores are accepted

Always check the exact requirements of your target institution or visa category. In general:

  • IELTS is especially common for UK, Australian, New Zealand, and many Canadian applications, as well as a large number of European programs.
  • TOEFL remains the dominant test for many universities in the United States and is widely accepted worldwide.
  • Most large universities now accept both. The deciding factor is often the minimum score they publish, not the test name.
Action step: Make a short list of your top 3–5 destinations and check their official English-language requirements page. Note the exact test and minimum score accepted.

2. Speaking format: the biggest practical difference

This is the difference that most strongly affects how candidates experience the test.

  • IELTS Speaking is a live conversation with a human examiner. It feels more natural to many people, but it can also feel more stressful because of the interpersonal element.
  • TOEFL Speaking is recorded. You speak into a microphone in response to prompts. There is no human examiner in the room. Some candidates find this less intimidating; others find it harder to stay natural without a listener.

If you perform better in real conversations, IELTS may suit you. If you prefer controlled, solitary speaking tasks, TOEFL may feel more comfortable.

3. Writing differences

  • IELTS Academic Writing includes a graph/chart/diagram description task plus an essay.
  • TOEFL Writing includes an integrated task (reading + listening + writing) and an independent essay.

Candidates who are comfortable summarizing information from multiple sources often prefer the TOEFL integrated task. Candidates who prefer a more traditional essay plus a visual description often prefer IELTS.

4. Listening and reading style

Both tests measure academic listening and reading, but the delivery differs:

  • TOEFL is fully computer-based and uses a variety of academic-style lectures and campus conversations.
  • IELTS Listening includes a range of accents and everyday as well as academic contexts. The Academic Reading section uses longer, denser texts.

If you are used to North American academic English, TOEFL may feel more familiar. If you have more exposure to British, Australian, or mixed accents, IELTS Listening can feel more natural.

5. Scoring and score validity

Both tests report scores that are typically valid for two years. Institutions set their own minimums. A “good” score is the one that meets or exceeds the requirement of your specific target, not a universal number.

Conversion tables between IELTS bands and TOEFL scores exist, but they are approximate. Do not rely on unofficial conversions when making high-stakes decisions.

How to decide

  1. List the institutions or immigration categories that matter to you and check their official requirements.
  2. If both tests are accepted, take a short practice section of each (especially Speaking and Writing) and notice which format feels more natural under timed conditions.
  3. Consider practical factors: test dates available in your city, cost, and how soon you need the results.
  4. Choose one primary test and prepare for it deliberately rather than splitting effort between both.

Final note

Neither test is objectively “easier.” The better test is the one that matches your destination requirements and the skills you can demonstrate most reliably under exam conditions. Prepare for the specific format you choose; general English improvement helps both, but targeted practice produces the largest score gains.
